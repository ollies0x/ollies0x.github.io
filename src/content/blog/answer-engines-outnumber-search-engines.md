Search stopped being a place and became a behaviour.

Ask yourself where a buyer researches a problem in 2026. Google, sure. But also ChatGPT mid-conversation. Perplexity with citations. Gemini baked into the phone. Claude when they want the long version. Copilot inside the tools they already pay for. Six front doors, same internet behind all of them.

Most websites are dressed for exactly one of those doors.

## The hidden cost

Here's the mechanism nobody bills you for: classic SEO optimises for retrieval by ranking. Answer engines optimise for retrieval by citation. Different games, different rules.

- A ranking is a position on a page. A citation is a mention inside an answer.
- A ranking gets a click, maybe 2-3% of impressions. A citation gets quoted, synthesised, and repeated across every follow-up question.
- Rankings decay in days when you stop investing. Citations compound, because models retrain on corpora and RAG systems re-crawl - being the source last quarter is why you're the source this quarter.

The cost isn't that answer engines replaced Google. They haven't. The cost is that teams are still spending 100% of budget on a channel that reaches maybe half the research moments, while the other half happens in a chat window their brand never appears in.

## The binary

You're either a source or you're a summary.

Sources get named. Summaries get paraphrased into someone else's answer with your words and none of your credit. "According to industry reports" is what happens to businesses that weren't citable.

## What actually makes you citable

This is the part where the fundamentals win again, because they always do:

1. **Answer the actual question, early.** Answer engines extract passages, not pages. If your best paragraph is 800 words below your intro essay, you've lost the extraction.
2. **Structure like a reference, not a story.** Clear headings, definitions, numbers with units, tables. Models cite text they can lift cleanly.
3. **Be crawlable to the machines that matter.** robots.txt welcoming GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot and friends. You'd be shocked how many sites block their own distribution while paying for ads.
4. **Say things worth quoting.** Original numbers, named frameworks, strong claims you can defend. "We analysed 147 audits and found X" beats "SEO is important" every single time.
5. **Exist as an entity.** Consistent name, schema markup, an about page that says who you are in plain language. The engines need to know you're a thing before they'll cite the thing.

None of this replaces technical SEO. Crawlability, speed, clean architecture - that's table stakes for both games. But passage-level clarity is the new on-page, and almost nobody is doing it deliberately.

## The diagnosis

Pull your analytics and ask one question: how much of your traffic arrived with a referrer you can't attribute? Zero-click searches were the warning shot. Zero-referrer answers are the main event.

If your content strategy still starts with "keyword volume", you're optimising for the door that's closing instead of the doors that are opening.

## What to do next

1. Pick your ten highest-intent pages and check whether an answer engine cites them. Ask ChatGPT and Perplexity your money questions tonight. Note who gets named.
2. Check robots.txt against the major AI crawlers. If you're blocking them, decide deliberately - don't let a default setting make the call.
3. Rewrite one page for extraction: answer first, evidence second, narrative last.
4. Add schema so the machines know what you are.
5. Publish one original number per month. Sources have data; summaries have adjectives.

The internet didn't stop searching. It stopped typing into a box with ten blue links. Dress for the doors people actually walk through.
